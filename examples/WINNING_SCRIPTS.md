# PROVEN AD SCRIPTS - WINNING PATTERNS

## HOOK PATTERNS THAT CONVERT:

### Breaking News Angle:
- "This was all over the news yesterday, but they buried it fast"
- "This was all over yesterday's news, so let me break it down before it disappears"
- "This just broke yesterday, but I need to explain it before it gets buried again"

### Direct Problem Call-Out:
- "If you're over 60 and still don't have final expense coverage, this is for you"
- "Most [audience] have no idea this exists"
- "[Audience] have until [deadline] to lock in rates"

### Urgency First:
- "Last chance for [audience] to lock this in"
- "The window closes tonight for seniors to claim this benefit"

## AGITATION PATTERNS (5-15 SECONDS):

### Create FOMO:
- "Once the window shuts, it may be too late"
- "After [deadline], Congress could shut this down forever"
- "Most Americans still have no idea this exists"

### Amplify Pain:
- "You're paying 40% in taxes while the wealthy pay nothing"
- "Every dollar you give the IRS is a dollar your family never sees"
- "If something happened tomorrow, could your family stay in their home?"

### Add Deadline:
- "The deadline to lock this in is [specific date]"
- "Rates don't last forever"
- "These benefits are running out fast"

## SCRIPT FRAMEWORKS (Use Different Ones Per Client/Script):

### FRAMEWORK SELECTION GUIDE:
| Framework | Best For | Audience Temp | Emotional Trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| HSO (Hook-Story-Offer) | Cold FB traffic, FE 55+ | Cold | Empathy, identification |
| PPPP (Promise-Picture-Proof-Push) | IUL working class | Cold/Warm | Aspiration, proof |
| QUEST | Retargeting, skeptics | Warm | Trust, logic, education |
| Testimonial-First | TikTok, UGC-style | Cold | Social proof, peer trust |
| SLAP | 15-30s short ads | Cold | Urgency, simplicity |
| Villain Framework | IUL, tax-frustrated | Cold/Warm | Anger, injustice, control |
| Curiosity Gap / Open Loop | YouTube pre-roll, skeptics | Cold | Intrigue, FOMO |
| SIC (Situation-Impact-Change) | FE post-loss audiences | Warm | Logic, inevitability |

---

### FRAMEWORK 1: HSO — HOOK, STORY, OFFER
**Structure:** Visceral hook → Compressed narrative (someone like them found it) → Offer as natural conclusion

**Final Expense Example:**
> **Hook:** "A family in this town had to pass around a bucket at their father's funeral just to cover the bill."
>
> **Story:** "Nobody had a plan. He'd meant to take care of it. He just kept putting it off. When he passed, the family had 48 hours to come up with $12,000 — and they didn't have it. They sold a car. They borrowed from neighbors. The grief became a money crisis instead of a goodbye. Months later, his wife found out there was a benefit for people his age. No medical exam. Payments as low as $30 a month. The entire thing covered — ahead of time. She wished she'd known."
>
> **Offer:** "Click below to see if your family qualifies. Two questions. Takes 30 seconds."

**Why It Works:** Story bypasses the "this is an ad" filter. By the time the offer arrives, the viewer has already emotionally agreed with the conclusion.

**Use When:** Cold audience. Final Expense on Facebook to 55+. First touch.

---

### FRAMEWORK 2: PPPP — PROMISE, PICTURE, PROOF, PUSH
**Structure:** Bold claim upfront → Paint life after the promise → Hard evidence → Strong CTA

**IUL Example:**
> **Promise:** "There's a way to build six figures in tax-free wealth on a working-class income — no 401k, no financial advisor, no risk."
>
> **Picture:** "Imagine ten years from now — the mortgage is handled, the kids have a fund, and there's a six-figure account sitting there that the IRS can't touch. Not because of luck. Because a decision was made today."
>
> **Proof:** "Working families have been using this strategy for years. A welder redirected $200 a month and built $180,000 in accessible, tax-free cash value over a decade."
>
> **Push:** "Click below. Answer two questions. See what that number could look like. No calls, no spam."

**Why It Works:** Promise hooks logic, Picture hooks emotion, Proof collapses objections, Push creates movement before doubt returns.

**Use When:** IUL for aspirational working-class buyers. Facebook video, YouTube mid-roll.

---

### FRAMEWORK 3: QUEST — QUALIFY, UNDERSTAND, EDUCATE, STIMULATE, TRANSITION
**Structure:** Screen audience → Show you know their world → Introduce as information (not pitch) → Layer urgency → Low-friction CTA

**Final Expense Example:**
> **Qualify:** "This is specifically for anyone over 60 who's been putting off burial coverage because they think it's too expensive or too complicated."
>
> **Understand:** "That fear makes sense. Most people looked into it once, got a number that felt too high, and never went back. Meanwhile, the family is the one who'll face that bill with zero warning."
>
> **Educate:** "What most people don't know: simplified issue policies now exist — no medical exam, no blood work — that lock in coverage based on current age. A healthy 64-year-old can often qualify for $15,000–$25,000 for less than the price of a daily coffee."
>
> **Stimulate:** "Rates are set by age at the time of application. The window for today's rate closes permanently with every passing month."
>
> **Transition:** "Click below. Answer two questions. See the actual rate — no commitment."

**Why It Works:** Earns trust before asking for action. Viewer feels personally addressed, then heard, then educated. CTA feels like the obvious next step.

**Use When:** Second-touch retargeting. YouTube. Skeptical audiences who've objected before.

---

### FRAMEWORK 4: TESTIMONIAL-FIRST (Social Proof Lead)
**Structure:** Result statement as hook → "I didn't believe it either" → Before state → Discovery → Specific proof → Soft offer

**IUL Example:**
> "Last year, the only retirement plan was Social Security and whatever was left at the end of the month. That wasn't a plan — that was hope.
>
> Then someone mentioned an IUL policy. Honestly thought it was a scam. Sat on it for two months. Finally clicked because the article wouldn't go away.
>
> That was 18 months ago. Right now there's a cash value account at $22,000 — untouched, tax-free, accessible whenever needed. The family is covered. For the first time, retirement doesn't feel like a countdown to poverty.
>
> If that situation sounds familiar — one paycheck, no backup, no plan — click below. Takes 30 seconds. Two questions. No one calls."

**Why It Works:** Peer voice carries more weight than authority. "I didn't believe it either" disarms skepticism before it forms.

**Use When:** TikTok, Facebook Reels, UGC-style. IUL for working-class 30-50. Also Final Expense from children's POV ("Dad passed. The only thing we didn't have to worry about was the bill.").

---

### FRAMEWORK 5: SLAP — STOP, LOOK, ACT, PURCHASE
**Structure:** Pattern interrupt → Focus attention → Make behavior feel tiny → Reframe buying as accessing

**Final Expense Example:**
> **Stop:** "Most seniors are overpaying for funeral coverage they'll never fully use — or have no coverage at all and don't know it."
>
> **Look:** "There's a benefit specifically for adults over 50 that covers up to $25,000 in burial costs — set up in under 60 seconds, no medical exam."
>
> **Act:** "Click below and answer two simple questions to see the exact coverage available."
>
> **Purchase:** "It's not a sales call. It's just a number — what's available, at what rate, right now. 30 seconds."

**Why It Works:** Engineered for short attention spans. Each beat does one job only. "Purchase" reframes buying as accessing — eliminates friction at the critical moment.

**Use When:** 15-30 second social ads. Cold top-of-funnel. Seniors on Facebook. Static image ads.

---

### FRAMEWORK 6: THE VILLAIN FRAMEWORK
**Structure:** Name the villain → Show how villain wins if nothing changes → Product = the weapon (viewer is the hero) → Battle plan → Success state → Failure callback → CTA

**IUL Example:**
> **Villain:** "The IRS gets a piece of every dollar put into a 401k. Every year. On the way in and on the way out. Most working families don't even know they're playing that game."
>
> **Villain Wins:** "The average American retires with $150,000 saved and $90,000 exposed to taxes they didn't plan for. The IRS wins by default — because nobody told them there was another option."
>
> **The Weapon:** "An IUL policy operates outside the tax system. The money grows tax-free, protected from loss, and accessed completely tax-free. The IRS can't touch it."
>
> **Battle Plan:** "One application. Two questions. A licensed specialist reviews the options. No commitment."
>
> **Success State:** "A family that locks this in today is looking at a tax-free account that grows for 10, 20, 30 years — untouched by market crashes, untouched by legislation."
>
> **CTA:** "Click below. Take the first step."

**Why It Works:** Humans organize around shared enemies. Removes shame (viewer isn't behind because they failed — the system was designed to keep them there). Creates anger (motivating) instead of shame (paralyzing).

**Use When:** IUL for tax-frustrated middle-income earners, business owners, politically-aware audiences. Also FE with insurance companies as the villain.

---

### FRAMEWORK 7: CURIOSITY GAP / OPEN LOOP
**Structure:** Open a loop (partial reveal) → Widen the gap → Stakes → Tease → Open second loop → Close loop 1 with solution → Close loop 2 with CTA

**Final Expense Example:**
> **Open Loop:** "There is a government-approved benefit for adults over 50 that most people never claim — and the window to lock in current rates closes soon."
>
> **Widen Gap:** "It's not a government program. Not a subsidy. Doesn't require paperwork, a doctor, or a phone call."
>
> **Stakes:** "Every month it goes unclaimed, the rate for that age bracket goes up. Some people find out at 68 and wish they'd known at 60."
>
> **Tease:** "It's a type of coverage specifically engineered for people who've been denied traditional policies — or who never thought coverage was affordable."
>
> **Second Loop:** "The reason most people haven't heard about this is something worth knowing about the insurance industry."
>
> **Close Loop 1:** "It's called a simplified issue final expense policy. No medical exam. Up to $25,000 in burial coverage. Locked in at today's rate."
>
> **CTA:** "Click below to find out why they don't advertise this — and what rate is available right now. Two questions. 30 seconds."

**Why It Works:** The brain can't stop watching when a loop is open — it's running a background process trying to resolve the question. Stacking two loops holds attention through the full script.

**Use When:** YouTube pre-roll (first 5 seconds must earn the next 30). TikTok. Skeptical audiences who need to feel they "discovered" something vs. being sold.

---

### FRAMEWORK 8: SIC — SITUATION, IMPACT, CHANGE
**Structure:** Specific snapshot of their daily reality → Concrete downstream consequence → One pivot decision → CTA tied to the pivot

**Final Expense Example:**
> **Situation:** "For most families, the plan when a parent passes is to 'figure it out.' That means calls to funeral homes at 2am, GoFundMe pages going up before the obituary, siblings arguing about money during the worst week of their lives."
>
> **Impact:** "The average funeral now costs $9,000–$12,000. Most families don't have that sitting in savings. So the grief becomes a financial crisis on top of an emotional one — and that doesn't go away for years."
>
> **Change:** "One decision changes the entire outcome. A final expense policy means the bill is already paid. The family doesn't make a single financial decision. They just grieve."
>
> **CTA:** "Click below. This is that decision. Two questions. 30 seconds. No calls, no spam."

**Why It Works:** Slow-burn realization framework. Doesn't alarm — escorts the viewer through a logical sequence that ends in an undeniable conclusion. Works on analytical viewers who distrust emotional manipulation.

**Use When:** Final Expense for audiences who've recently experienced loss. IUL for retirement gap messaging. Analytical or skeptical audiences.

## HOOK STYLE LIBRARY (Beyond Breaking News):

### Current Proven Hooks:
- Breaking News Angle (news + conspiracy) ✅ Keep using
- Direct Problem Call-Out ✅ Keep using
- Urgency First ✅ Keep using

### Additional Hook Styles:

**1. The Confession Hook** *(disarming, peer-voice)*
Opens with an admission that lowers defenses and builds instant empathy.
- "For years, the only retirement plan was hoping Social Security would be enough. That's not a plan."
- "My uncle passed last year and the first call the family had to make was to a bank, not a funeral home."
- Best for: TikTok, UGC-style Facebook, Final Expense

**2. The Counterintuitive / Myth-Busting Hook** *(cognitive dissonance)*
Directly contradicts a widely-held belief. Brain must resolve it.
- "Having a 401k doesn't mean retirement is covered. For most working families, it means owing the IRS on the way out."
- "Saving money is keeping most families broke. Here's why."
- Best for: IUL, educated audiences, YouTube

**3. The Statistic-Shock Hook** *(involuntary processing)*
A number so specific and unexpected that stopping is involuntary.
- "The average American family has 72 hours to come up with $9,000 after an unexpected death. Most can't."
- "One in four families who lose a parent go into debt within 30 days of the funeral."
- Best for: Final Expense, Facebook, YouTube

**4. The Identity Mirror Hook** *(self-identification)*
Calls out exactly who the viewer is — not demographics, but identity.
- "If working hard for 30 years and still feeling financially behind sounds familiar — this is about what changed that."
- "For anyone who's always handled everything for everyone else and never set anything aside for their own family."
- "If the plan is to 'figure out the funeral stuff later' — this matters."
- Best for: IUL for blue-collar, Final Expense for caretakers

**5. The "What Nobody Tells You" Hook** *(suppressed information)*
Positions the information as deliberately hidden.
- "Nobody in the financial industry tells working families about this because there's no commission in it for them."
- "What most agents skip over when showing coverage options."
- Best for: IUL, conspiracy-receptive audiences, Facebook, TikTok

**6. The Specific Scenario Hook** *(pattern interrupt)*
A hyper-specific scenario so concrete the viewer must evaluate whether it applies to them.
- "68 years old. No final expense coverage. Kids would have to figure out the $12,000 funeral. That's the plan?"
- "If a paycheck stops tomorrow, how long does the family stay in the house?"
- Best for: Final Expense, older Facebook audiences

**7. The Aspiration / Legacy Hook** *(pulls instead of pushes)*
Opens not with pain but with what the viewer wants to become or leave behind.
- "The families that have real wealth 20 years from now all made one specific financial move — most of them made it before 45."
- "The goal isn't to get rich. It's to make sure the family never has to choose between grief and a GoFundMe."
- Best for: IUL for young families and business owners, legacy-minded seniors

## ALTERNATIVE CTA APPROACHES (Rotate These):

### Core CTA (Keep as Primary):
- "Click below. Answer two simple questions. No calls, no spam." ✅

### Rotation Options:

**Quiz Funnel CTA:**
- "Answer 3 quick questions to see exact coverage options and price — no personal info until choosing to share it."
- Why: Interactive funnels feel like tools, not ads. Converts 2-4x higher than static forms.

**Calculator CTA:**
- "Use the free coverage calculator below — put in age and budget, it shows exactly what's available."
- Why: Provides immediate value before asking for anything.

**Peer-Language CTA:**
- "Honestly, just click and see the number. If it doesn't work, nothing lost."
- "It took 2 minutes. Worst case, more information. Best case, the family is covered."
- Why: Sounds like advice from a peer who already did it, not a sales pitch.

**Stakes-Based CTA:**
- "Click before the rate locks in at next month's higher number."
- "The next time this comes up might be the one time it's too late to act."
- Why: Connects urgency to inaction cost — more honest, more durable than artificial deadlines.

**Enhanced "See If You Qualify" CTA:**
- "Click below. Two questions. See if qualifying is possible — no commitment, no call required."
- "Takes 30 seconds. See the actual coverage amount and rate available right now. No one calls unless requested."

## FINAL EXPENSE vs. IUL — EMOTIONAL TRIGGER CHEAT SHEET:

| Dimension | Final Expense | IUL |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary Emotion** | Guilt, love, duty, fear of burdening family | Frustration, aspiration, injustice, hope |
| **Core Fear** | "My kids will have to figure out the bill" | "I'll work my whole life and still end up broke" |
| **Aspiration** | "My family won't have to scramble" | "I'll build something they didn't expect" |
| **Time Horizon** | Immediate ("if I died tonight") | Future ("in 10-20 years") |
| **Hook Angle** | Protection, duty, urgency of NOW | Opportunity, frustration with current path |
| **Villain** | High funeral costs, unprepared families | The IRS, 401k limits, big financial institutions |
| **Social Proof Voice** | "My daughter won't have to pay a thing" | "I built $100k in tax-free wealth on a truck driver's salary" |
| **Solution Framing** | SHIELD — protects from disaster | VEHICLE — builds something new |
| **Urgency Type** | Age/health-based ("rates go up every year") | Opportunity-based ("window to start young is closing") |
| **CTA Tone** | Gentle, protective ("make sure they're covered") | Energizing, activating ("start building today") |
| **Script Length** | 45-55 seconds (emotion hits fast) | 55-75 seconds (needs more explanation) |
| **B-Roll Emotion** | Family warmth, comfort, intergenerational love | Working hard, tax docs, relief, financial success |

**Critical Distinction:**
- Final Expense = NOW purchase. Fear is immediate. Script makes viewer feel their unprotected state RIGHT NOW.
- IUL = FUTURE purchase. Fear is arriving at retirement with nothing. Script must make the future vivid enough that pain of inaction TODAY exceeds friction of acting.
- This is why IUL hooks about death ("if something happened tonight") underperform vs. hooks about retirement poverty ("if nothing changes in 20 years").

## KEY PRINCIPLES:

1. **Hooks create urgency through news angle or direct problem**
2. **Agitation amplifies pain + adds deadline**
3. **Social proof = skepticism → belief arc with specific examples**
4. **Solution focuses on simple benefits with numbers**
5. **Authority uses conspiracy angle (us vs big companies)**
6. **CTA removes friction + adds final urgency**
7. **NO first-person credentials or sales pitch language**
8. **Pure emotion, urgency, benefits**
