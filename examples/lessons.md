## CORRECTIONS LOG

### March 19, 2026 - Urgency Deadlines Must Be Immediate (Not Future Dates)

**Problem:** Scripts used "December 31st" deadline which is 9 months away, killing urgency.

**Bad Example:** "The window to lock this in closes December 31st."

**Why It's Bad:** Future dates don't create urgency. Gives them permission to procrastinate.

**Good Examples:**
- "The window closes tonight"
- "The window closes soon"
- "Rates change tomorrow"
- "This opportunity ends today"

**Best Example (from Script 3):** "Blue collar workers have until tonight to lock in..."

**Rule:** NEVER use specific future dates (December 31st, end of year, etc). Use immediate urgency language: "tonight", "today", "soon", "before it's too late".

---

### March 19, 2026 - Always Say "IUL Policy" Not "Indexed Policy"

**Problem:** Scripts said "It's called an indexed policy" which is vague and confusing.

**Bad Example:** "It's called an indexed policy"

**Good Example:** "It's called an IUL policy"

**Why:** IUL is the actual product name. "Indexed policy" sounds generic and doesn't build brand recognition.

**Rule:** Always say "IUL policy" in solution section. Never use "indexed policy", "indexed universal coverage", or other variations.

### March 19, 2026 - IUL vs Final Expense Benefits (Don't Mix Them)

**Problem:** IUL scripts focused too much on death benefits (Final Expense angle) instead of wealth building + protection.

**IUL Core Benefits (in order of priority):**
1. Tax-free wealth building (PRIMARY - lead with this)
2. Access cash anytime (liquidity, flexibility)
3. Family protection if something happens (death benefit as SECONDARY)

**Final Expense Core Benefits (in order of priority):**
1. Death benefit to cover funeral costs (PRIMARY - lead with this)
2. Family won't scramble for money
3. Peace of mind

**Winning IUL Script Pattern:**
- Hook: News angle
- Agitation: "You can build tax-free wealth while protecting your family"
- Social Proof: "I qualified in under 60 seconds. My wife won't have to worry."
- Solution: "You're building cash you can access anytime. Tax-free, no penalties. And if something happens, your family gets a lump sum."

**Rule:** For IUL, lead with WEALTH BUILDING first, family protection second. For Final Expense, lead with DEATH BENEFIT first. Don't mix the messaging.

### March 19, 2026 - Hooks Must Be Specific and Compelling (Not Vague)

**Problem:** Hooks ended with vague phrases like "this is going to hit hard" which don't create urgency.

**Bad Hook:** "If you're a blue collar worker with no backup plan for your family, this is going to hit hard."

**Why It's Bad:** "Hit hard" is vague. Doesn't tell them WHAT will hit hard or WHY they should care.

**Good Examples:**
- "If you're a blue collar worker making $50K+ with no backup plan, your family is one accident away from losing everything."
- "What I'm about to show you could change everything for your family's financial future."

**Rule:** Hooks must be SPECIFIC about the pain or the opportunity. Avoid vague endings like "this is going to hit hard", "you need to see this", "this is important". Tell them EXACTLY what's at stake.

### March 19, 2026 - Authority Language: Emphasize HIDING Not "Cheap Insurance"

**Problem:** Authority section said "sell you cheap insurance" which sounds contradictory.

**Bad Example:** "Big companies sell you cheap insurance that expires when you need it most."

**Why It's Bad:** "Cheap" makes it sound like a good deal. We want to position them as HIDING the good option.

**Good Examples:**
- "Big companies hide this from you when you need it most"
- "They bury this option because they can't make money off it"
- "They push other products because this one doesn't line their pockets"

**Rule:** Authority section should emphasize HIDING/BURYING the good option, not selling cheap alternatives.

### March 19, 2026 - Use Aspirational Urgency Language (Product-Specific)

**Problem:** Hooks used "emergency family benefits" which isn't compelling for cold audience.

**Bad Example:** "Blue collar workers have until tonight to lock in emergency family benefits"

**Why It's Bad:** "Emergency benefits" sounds like government aid or welfare. Not aspirational.

**Good Examples (based on product):**
- **IUL:** "Once in a lifetime wealth building opportunity"
- **IUL:** "Generational wealth building window closing"
- **Final Expense:** "Final chance to secure family funeral protection"

**Rule:** Use aspirational urgency language that matches the product. IUL = wealth/opportunity language. Final Expense = protection/security language.

### March 19, 2026 - Limit Location-Based Social Proof to ONE Script Maximum

**Problem:** All 3 scripts used "A mechanic in Michigan", "A welder in Ohio" format. Felt repetitive.

**Why It's Bad:** Loses authenticity when every script uses same format.

**Solution (Vary Structure Across Scripts):**
- **Script 1:** Use location-based examples ("A welder in Ohio said...")
- **Script 2:** Use anonymous group examples ("Blue collar workers thought this was fake...")
- **Script 3:** Use first-person testimonial style ("'I didn't believe it either, but I qualified in 60 seconds'")

**Rule:** Only use "A [job] in [state]" format in ONE script per generation. Vary social proof structure across the 3 scripts.

### March 19, 2026 - Don't Mention Life Insurance Product Names Early

**Problem:** Scripts mentioned "life insurance" and "term insurance" in hooks/agitation, killing curiosity.

**Bad Example:** "Your life insurance? It's probably term that expires worthless."

**Why It's Bad:** Cold market doesn't understand life insurance products. Mentioning it early triggers resistance.

**Rule:** NEVER mention "life insurance", "term insurance", "whole life", or any insurance product names in Hook, Agitation, or Social Proof sections. Only talk about WHAT IT DOES (tax-free wealth, family protection). Product name goes in Solution section only.

---

### March 19, 2026 - Don't Mention Specific States Unless Provided

**Problem:** Scripts mentioned "Texas", "Dallas", "Houston" even though no state was provided.

**Why It's Bad:** Makes it seem templated. User might be targeting national audience or different state.

**Rule:** NEVER include specific state/city names unless user provides --states parameter. Use "across the country", "nationwide", "working families everywhere" instead.

---

### March 19, 2026 - Don't Mention Tax Code Sections

**Problem:** Scripts mentioned "Section 7702" which sounds like a loophole.

**Bad Example:** "It's called Section 7702 of the tax code."

**Why It's Bad:** Sounds shady like a tax loophole instead of legitimate financial product.

**Rule:** NEVER mention tax code sections (7702, etc). Focus on benefits: "tax-free growth", "tax-free access", "tax-free inheritance".

### March 19, 2026 - Stop Comparing to Other Insurance Products

**Problem:** Scripts compared IUL to term insurance, whole life, etc.

**Bad Example:** "Term life insurance is the biggest scam" or "Unlike term that expires worthless"

**Why It's Bad:** Feels salesy and defensive. Cold audience doesn't care about product comparisons.

**Rule:** NEVER compare to other insurance products. NEVER say "unlike X" or "better than Y". Pure benefit focus only.

### March 19, 2026 - Hooks Must Be Purely Emotional (No Product Talk)

**Problem:** Hooks assumed they had coverage or mentioned products.

**Bad Hook:** "You're probably paying for worthless protection that expires when you need it most."

**Good Hooks (from user):**
- "Right now, your family is one accident away from financial disaster, and the worst part is that you're not prepared at all"
- "My uncle passed away last week and we had to scramble to get the funds for his funeral"

**Why Good Hooks Work:** Pure emotion. Relatable story. No product talk. Just pain.

**Rule:** Hooks must invoke PURE EMOTION (fear, frustration, loss). NO product mentions. NO assumptions about what they have.

### March 19, 2026 - Generate Scripts With Varied Frameworks

**Problem:** Two Final Expense clients received nearly identical scripts — same hook style, same structure, same flow. Scripts felt copy-pasted with audience details swapped.

**Why It's Bad:** When every script uses the same Breaking News → Agitation → Skepticism Social Proof → Solution → "Why Haven't You Heard" → 2-question CTA structure, the output is predictable and clients can tell they're getting a template.

**Rule:** Each generation of 3 scripts MUST use 3 DIFFERENT frameworks from WINNING_SCRIPTS.md. Never use the same structural framework for more than 1 script per client in the same niche.

**Framework Variety Matrix (enforce this):**
- Script 1: Breaking News / Conspiracy (existing proven pattern)
- Script 2: One of — HSO, PPPP, Testimonial-First, Curiosity Gap
- Script 3: One of — Villain, QUEST, SIC, SLAP

**Hook Variety Matrix (enforce this):**
- Script 1: Breaking news hook
- Script 2: One of — Confession, Counterintuitive, Statistic-Shock, Identity Mirror
- Script 3: One of — Specific Scenario, Aspiration/Legacy, "What Nobody Tells You"

**CTA Variety:**
- At least 1 of the 3 scripts should test an alternative CTA (peer-language, stakes-based, quiz funnel) — not all 3 on the same "click below, 2 questions" wording.

**Before generating:** Name the framework and hook style for each of the 3 scripts. If all 3 are the same → stop and diversify.
